THE PROBLEM

What is it?

Household waste includes food scraps, packaging, paper, plastics, glass and other items thrown away during everyday life.

When different kinds of waste are mixed together, useful materials can become difficult to recover.

Where is it?

Waste problems can be found in homes, streets, overflowing bins, landfills, waterways and public spaces.

Inadequate collection and disposal systems can make the problem worse.

Why & how does it occur?

Household waste increases when people buy more than they need, use disposable products, throw away repairable items and fail to separate waste correctly.

Food waste, contaminated recyclables and mixed materials can make recovery more difficult. Some waste is sent to landfills, while poorly managed waste can pollute the surrounding environment.

What can help? Buy thoughtfully, reuse what you already own, separate waste at home, compost suitable organic waste and follow local disposal rules.

Who is it affecting?

Waste pollution affects communities, sanitation workers, wildlife, ecosystems and future generations.

Communities near poorly managed disposal sites may face additional environmental and health risks.

Recycling matters, but preventing waste in the first place can be even better.

Try borrowing instead of buying, sharing things you rarely use, repairing broken objects and planning meals to reduce food waste.

YOUR MINI CRAFT GUIDE

Every project starts with something you might otherwise throw away. Choose clean, safe materials and adapt the design to what you have at home.

01 · Bottle Planter

Materials: A clean plastic bottle, soil, suitable seeds or a small plant, and water.

Steps: Plan the container, ask an adult to help prepare any openings, add suitable growing material, then care for the plant.

Why reuse it? It gives a discarded container another purpose.

02 · Cardboard Organiser

Materials: Clean cardboard, a ruler, pencil, glue and decorative paper scraps.

Steps: Sketch compartments, ask an adult to help cut pieces if needed, assemble the organiser and decorate it.

Why reuse it? It turns packaging into useful storage.

03 · Scrap Paper Collage

Materials: Used paper, old magazines, a backing sheet and non-toxic glue.

Steps: Plan a picture, arrange the scraps, glue them down and let the work dry.

Why reuse it? It makes creative use of paper that is no longer needed.

Safety first! Use clean materials only. Have an adult supervise cutting, heated tools and any work involving sharp edges. Do not reuse chemical containers, damaged glass or packaging that held hazardous substances. Wash your hands after crafting.

KNOW THE RULES

Recycling rules differ between countries and even between cities. Check official guidance before putting an item in a recycling bin.

🇮🇳 India

India's Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016 established a framework for waste segregation, collection and management. Check current national and local requirements, including any updated rules and municipal instructions.

Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change ↗

Swachh Bharat Mission ↗

🇺🇸 United States

Recycling acceptance varies by local authority and waste collection provider. Some communities accept certain plastics and paper products, while others do not.

US EPA recycling guidance ↗

🇬🇧 United Kingdom

Household collection instructions can vary by local council. Check your council's guidance for packaging, food waste, glass and other household materials.

UK government information ↗

🇪🇺 European Union

The EU has waste and circular-economy legislation, while national systems and local collection arrangements determine how households separate many materials.

European Commission: waste and recycling ↗

🇦🇺 Australia

Waste collection arrangements differ across states, territories and councils. Local guidance is important for knowing what belongs in each bin.

Australian government waste information ↗

🌍 General recycling tips

  • Separate materials according to local instructions.
  • Keep recyclable items free from food residue where required.
  • Never place batteries, chemicals or electronic waste in ordinary recycling bins unless specifically instructed.
  • Reuse and repair items before discarding them.
